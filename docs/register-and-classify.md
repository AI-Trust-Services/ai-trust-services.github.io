# Register & Classify an AI System

Every governance journey on the platform begins with registration. An **Application Owner** or **AI Engineer** brings an AI-enabled application into the platform, records its context, and attaches the documentation that later workflows draw on. This page follows that journey and the views you work with along the way.

## Registering a system

Registration starts from the **Register an AI System** action on your landing page or from the registry. The registration form captures the use-case context that drives everything downstream:

- **Name and description** of the AI-enabled application.
- **Intended purpose** — what the system is for.
- **Use in the European Union** and **territory of usage**.
- **Intended users** and deployment context.

You can also set up collaboration directly during registration:

- **Assign contributors** so the right people can help complete the assessment.
- **Upload supporting documentation** — up to ten documents, with per-file progress shown as each upload completes.

On registration the system receives a unique identifier (`SYS-XXXXXXXX`) and enters the registry. The documents you upload are not just stored — they are indexed so that later AI-assisted workflows can read and reuse their content (see [AI-Assisted EU AI Act Assessment](/docs/ai-assisted-assessment)).

## The AI System Registry

The registry is the central inventory of every AI-enabled application in scope. It presents systems in a table view where you can scan, sort, and filter across your portfolio, and open any system to see its full detail.

## The system detail view

Opening a system brings you to a structured detail view that acts as the hub for everything you do with that system. It is organised into tabs:

- **Overview** — system details, current risk classification status, progress through the governance process, and recent audit activity. From here you start assessments, upload further files, edit system details, or remove the system.
- **Assessments** — all ongoing and completed assessments for the system, with their status and key properties.
- **Files** — the documentation attached to the system.

## Model cards

For systems built on specific models, a dedicated **model card** view captures the relevant model attributes in one place. Model cards give technical and compliance stakeholders a consistent description of the underlying model — and their presence (or absence) is one of the signals the platform watches for in its [alert rules](/docs/how-it-works#alerts).

## Lifecycle stages

Each registered system carries a **lifecycle stage** that reflects where it is operationally — from Development through Testing, Conformity, Market, Post-Market, and finally Decommissioned. The stage is visible on the system record and provides context for assessments, monitoring, and alerting. See the [Glossary](/docs/how-it-works#glossary) for the full definition.

## Next: assessment

Registration captures *what* the system is. The next step is to determine *how it is classified* under the EU AI Act — a guided, AI-assisted flow covered in [AI-Assisted EU AI Act Assessment](/docs/ai-assisted-assessment).
